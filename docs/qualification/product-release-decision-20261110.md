# npm v3 PR review candidate: qualification decision

The exact candidate at `c140aed0cfc8ed30890d9a01766d2a8542bc9c21` is **not qualified for a versioned release** under the frozen product gates. The third repository-disjoint sample completed 17 of 30 historical npm v3 update reviews, below the 80% end-to-end floor. It completed 17 of 18 independently label-complete reviews, below the separate 95% conditional floor. The independent labels were frozen before candidate replay. The full local inputs, logs, and five-round outputs are retained under ignored `ecosystem_audit/product-release-20261110/holdout-next`; the tracked [compact receipt](evidence/product-release-decision-20261110.json) records their hashes and the source-bound decision.

## What passed

- All 150 processes across five rounds exited with the expected protocol status, no timeout, and byte-identical output for each of the 30 cases. There were zero fact mismatches on qualified records and zero unexpected allowed outcomes.
- The candidate matched 9,926 of 9,985 independently assessable events. All 59 misses came from one case, `Makumiii/fikra` PR #28. Its base lock omits four dependencies declared by an optional wasm package, so the graph is incomplete. Its expanded archive evidence also crosses the existing 4 GiB per-snapshot limit. Raising that limit alone would not make the dependency route complete. The tool reported this case as incomplete.
- The exact candidate binary SHA-256 is `89fe1f265f2b31ee9416a5c3f5d838e435f889037e9ecfa0cb940f956a3e96c5`. The independent evidence matrix passed 333/333 cases, the trusted Git wrapper passed 20/20 controls, and the native npm comparison passed 17/17 authored loopback cases. The tar ASan gate passed five uninterrupted 60-second runs, 8,740,484 inputs total, below the 512 MiB RSS cap. CI, CodeQL, and the reproducible signed Linux candidate workflow passed on the same source commit. Public package lifecycle scripts were never executed.
- The owned Earthquake Lab pilot ran against a real Renovate Vite update with 45 qualified records per side and zero unknowns. A separate local Git-object pilot demonstrated an exact blocked-to-allowed approval with unchanged non-policy facts.

## Why the coverage gate failed

The 30 PRs came from 30 distinct repositories and were retained regardless of the result. The independent oracle could completely label only 18. Its 12 partial pairs contain 263 unknown package-side occurrences: 97 missing evidence locators, 156 children that were not directly declared bundled dependencies, four archives over the 10,000-member bound, two manifest-version disagreements, two duplicate or case-folded archive paths, and two manifest/lock development-dependency disagreements. These are occurrence counts across base and head, not 263 distinct packages or PRs. A current registry lookup cannot create historical byte provenance for a missing locator. The oracle ceiling was therefore 18/30 even before product replay.

The one additional product-incomplete case among the 18 label-complete pairs is the `fikra` case above. Its missing lock dependency routes are a separate reason for an incomplete report. The 4 GiB cap accounts for its 59 missed assessable byte and manifest events. The other 12 product-incomplete cases had no assessable event misses; their unknowns or unsupported archive facts remained visible. These results preserve the failed 80% and 95% gates rather than changing the denominator after inspection.

Acquisition accounted for 9.60 GB of source and archive inputs and took 338 seconds, with 3,518 new registry downloads and 3,502 read-only cache reuses. The five-round local replay used three concurrent workers. The median of per-case median process times was 26.18 seconds; the slowest process took 83.03 seconds and the largest process RSS was 1,045,331,968 bytes. These process costs exclude acquisition and are not a comparison against an equally provisioned ordinary review.

## Release decision

Keep [PR #34](https://github.com/steph4n-gh/tau-gate/pull/34) in draft. The local controls support a bounded exact-evidence reviewer for inputs with complete provenance; they do not support the frozen broad-coverage release claim. A hosted positive comment-approval transition still needs a PR authored by someone other than its approving human maintainer. The prepared five-maintainer blinded comparison has not run, so reviewer advantage and time savings remain unmeasured. No release tag or consumer rollout is justified by this result.

The next candidate should define its supported-input boundary before selection, retain unsupported PRs in an intake denominator, and use a new unseen set to test that boundary. Historical missing bytes and undeclared bundles must stay unknown. A controlled hosted approval transition and actual maintainer study are still required before claiming the review workflow is a qualified product.
